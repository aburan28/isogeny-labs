Thesis

The move to post-quantum cryptography will replace the public-key cryptography in nearly every system in use. The new schemes are being deployed in new code against fixed deadlines, on cost estimates that have had little time to be tested, and that is why this lab exists.

Cryptographic hardness is estimated once and re-examined rarely. Someone computes the cost of the best known attack, publishes the figure, and the field cites it for decades. The algorithm is usually sound. The cost model ages, against hardware that does not stand still and against cryptanalysis that does not stop.

Most of the time nobody checks. Checking is engineering rather than mathematics, it produces no theorem, and the incentives in this field do not reward it. So the numbers that parameter choices, migration plans and procurement deadlines all rest on go unexamined for years at a time.

This lab builds the attacks and measures them. We implement known cryptanalytic methods against elliptic curve and isogeny based systems, report what they cost on hardware that exists now, and publish the code and the measurements so the figures can be checked by anyone who wants to. Where the estimate and the measurement disagree, the measurement is the finding.

Research

  1. A 29-year-old challenge got 17 times cheaper, and the math didn't change

    GPU cryptanalysis · parallel Pollard rho · CUDA

    Certicom's ECC2K-130 challenge has been open since 1997. We made the standard attack on it run 17.1 times faster on one NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell without changing the algorithm, which cuts its expected cost from about 80 GPU-years to 4.7.

  2. We spent eleven weeks pointing AI agents at cryptanalysis. Our tests kept passing code we knew was broken.

    Language-model agents · research harness · verification

    Eleven weeks of language-model agents doing cryptanalysis research, in a system built to catch confident wrong answers. There was no cryptanalytic advance. The finding is about verification: our own checker passed code we had deliberately broken, five times over, once because the code noticed it was being tested.

  4. We rented a few hundred machines and spent four weeks attacking a 29-year-old challenge

    Pollard rho · GPUs, CPUs and an FPGA · a distributed campaign

    We rebuilt the planned 2009 attack on Certicom's ECC2K-130 from scratch and ran it on rented GPUs, CPUs and an FPGA. After 486 million stored points, about 5.4% of the expected work, there is no collision, as the mathematics predicts. Finishing is a question of money, roughly $56,000 of GPU time at spot prices, not of mathematics.
